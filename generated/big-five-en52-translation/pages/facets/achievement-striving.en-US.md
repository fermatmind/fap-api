# Achievement Striving Facet: How Strongly Do You Pursue High Standards?

Achievement Striving concerns attraction to demanding goals, improvement, and standards that require sustained effort. It describes motivational direction—not success, worth, talent, productivity, income, or whether a goal deserves pursuit.

## Precise Definition

A stronger tendency may set ambitious targets and experience progress as energizing. A weaker tendency may prefer “good enough,” protect nonwork priorities, or invest deeply only in selected goals. Outcomes also depend on skill, health, opportunity, resources, discrimination, and goal quality.

## Parent Domain

Achievement Striving belongs to [Conscientiousness](/en/personality/big-five/conscientiousness), where standards can organize effort over time.

## Distinguishing Neighboring Facets

- **Achievement Striving versus Self-Discipline:** Ambition chooses a demanding destination; [Self-Discipline](/en/personality/big-five/facets/self-discipline) helps continue unpleasant steps.
- **Achievement Striving versus Competence:** [Competence](/en/personality/big-five/facets/competence) concerns expected capability, not desired level of accomplishment.
- **Achievement Striving versus perfectionism:** Perfectionism can involve rigid error concerns and self-worth contingencies. A facet score cannot establish it.

## Behavioral Cues

A stronger tendency may appear as stretch goals, tracking progress, seeking challenge, or difficulty resting without accomplishment. A weaker tendency may appear as bounded goals, stable satisfaction, selective effort, or declining status competition. Economic pressure and role demands can mimic ambition.

## Contextual Differences

Someone may strive intensely in art but not career hierarchy, or at work but not sport. Burnout, illness, caregiving, and blocked opportunity alter visible pursuit. Compare chosen standards across meaningful domains.

## Possible Expressions When Stronger

- Setting a target beyond minimum requirements.
- Seeking feedback that supports improvement.
- Linking time to measurable progress.
- Escalating standards after success.
- Neglecting recovery or relationships when goals dominate.

## Possible Expressions When Weaker

- Defining sufficiency and stopping there.
- Preserving leisure, relationships, or stability over status.
- Avoiding externally imposed competition.
- Underinvesting when a meaningful opportunity needs sustained stretch.
- Choosing depth or care without public achievement markers.

## Strengths and Costs

Stronger Achievement Striving can support sustained development and challenging projects. Possible costs include overwork, chronic dissatisfaction, narrow identity, and pursuing a harmful metric. Weaker Achievement Striving can protect balance and contentment; possible costs include missed opportunities or underdeveloped goals. Neither level ranks a life.

## An Original Case

A researcher sets demanding publication goals but also defines nonnegotiable sleep and family time. Ambition structures work without becoming a total identity. Success still depends on many factors beyond the facet.

## An Original Counterexample

A skilled craftsperson rejects promotion, maintains a modest workload, and produces excellent work. Low interest in status and stretch targets does not mean low ability, care, or growth.

## Self-Observation Questions

1. Who chose your most demanding current standard?
2. Does progress energize you, relieve anxiety, or protect self-worth?
3. What would “enough” look like?
4. Which goal deserves greater effort, and which should be retired?
5. What costs rise when the target escalates?

## Low-Risk Action Experiment

Select one goal and write its purpose, minimum useful outcome, stretch outcome, and stop rule. Track effort and recovery for seven days. The experiment tests goal design, not personal value.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Does strong Achievement Striving guarantee success?**

No. Outcomes depend on many factors, and this package provides no individual prediction.

**Does weaker Achievement Striving mean lacking ambition or growth?**

It may reflect selective priorities, sufficiency, or noncompetitive goals. Conduct and chosen aims need direct examination.

**How is Achievement Striving different from perfectionism?**

High standards can exist without rigid error concerns or self-worth dependence. A facet does not diagnose perfectionism.

**Can this tendency change?**

Goals and habits can change. This page does not forecast a stable score trajectory.

**How can I support a highly achievement-striving partner or friend?**

Ask what support is wanted, respect autonomy, notice recovery needs, and avoid making output the only measure of the relationship.
