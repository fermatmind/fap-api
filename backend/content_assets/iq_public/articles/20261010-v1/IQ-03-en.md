## How to Prepare Before Answering

To make the results of this assessment easier to interpret:

1. Allocate about 20 minutes and try to complete it in one session;
2. Use a device with clear graphic display and a stable internet connection;
3. Avoid taking the test when extremely fatigued, under the influence of alcohol, ill, or frequently interrupted;
4. Do not use search functions, screenshot recognition, assistance from others, or external problem-solving tools;
5. Remember this is a self-observation exercise—there's no need to repeatedly refresh in order to achieve higher scores.

If the environment is clearly unsuitable, please reschedule for another time. Treating a single instance of distraction as a fixed ability typically misleads oneself more than simply stopping.

## Strategies You Can Use While Answering

### First Look at Individual Changes

Check separately for quantity, position, orientation, size, color, or shape. Do not assume from the outset that complex rules exist.

### Check Rows and Columns Simultaneously

If a pattern can only explain one row but not the others, caution is warranted. Prefer a solution that applies the same rule to all known units.

### Use Elimination and Record Your Reasoning

Don't judge solely on "it looks like." Explain precisely why each candidate doesn't fit, to reduce intuitive misjudgments.

### Choose an Option and Continue When Uncertain

The current answering interface requires a selected option before you can continue to the next question. Select an answer for all 30 questions, then submit through the interface and view the result. If you are stuck, check the known cells again and eliminate candidates that contradict a clear feature. If uncertainty remains, choose the option best supported by the evidence you have, then continue. Complete certainty need not be a condition for proceeding, and one guess does not establish an ability level.

### An Original Synthetic Example: Check a Rule Against a Counterexample

This teaching example was created for this article. It is not a question or solution from the FermatMind test. Each cell contains circles of the same size:

| Row | First cell | Second cell | Third cell |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | ○ | ○○ | ○○○ |
| 2 | ○○ | ○○○ | ○○○○ |
| 3 | ○○○ | ○○○○ | ? |

Looking only at row 1, you might propose that the third count is the sum of the first two: 1 + 2 = 3. Row 2 is a counterexample: 2 + 3 would give 5, but its known third cell contains 4 circles. The proposed rule does not explain the whole grid.

Try a revised rule: each step to the right adds one circle. Both completed rows fit. Checking down the columns also gives an increase of one, so the missing cell should contain 5 circles. The extra check is useful because it exposes a rule that worked only by coincidence in one row. An option containing 5 triangles would match the count but contradict the consistent use of circles in this example.

This is not a universal formula for matrix problems. If a rule fails elsewhere, identify the specific contradiction and reconsider orientation, position, or combinations. Do not change the rule arbitrarily to protect a guess. If several candidates still fit, retain that uncertainty and choose on the available evidence; this method does not guarantee a unique answer.

### If Graphics or Results Have a Technical Problem

If a graphic is unclear or incomplete, check the display and connection before treating a guess as evidence about reasoning. A disabled next-question control before an option is selected is part of the current response requirement: select an option to continue. If you still cannot see the graphic adequately, do not force a completion for its own sake. This guide does not promise that leaving will preserve your progress.

If the result says, "This attempt could not be scored. Please complete the test again," follow that message; an unscored attempt is not a score of zero. If results have not appeared or a field shows a dash or "Not available yet," use the page's status information. Do not invent a population IQ or percentile to fill the gap.

## What Do Results Tell You?

Within the bounds of publicly available evidence, it can help you ask these questions:

- Which kinds of visual patterns do I recognize most quickly?
- Which kinds of rules tempt me to jump to conclusions?
- Does time pressure noticeably reduce my accuracy?
- Do I eliminate options systematically, or rely mostly on intuition?
- After reviewing an error, can I identify what went wrong?

The result cannot safely establish any of the following:

- Your "true IQ" is a fixed number;
- You are smarter than a fixed percentage of people;
- You are suited or unsuited for a particular career, school, or major;
- Your future income, achievements, or learning ceiling;
- The presence of cognitive or health issues.

Read the displayed performance first, and keep the published algorithm separate from the actual result fields. Your recollections are personal observations; a total score cannot establish item-by-item speed or error causes.

### The Beta Calculation Is Separate from the Result Display

The [current test entry](/en/tests/iq-test-intelligence-quotient-assessment) publishes a simulated random-response baseline: μ = 5.096, σ = 2.034, and z = (R − 5.096) ÷ 2.034. Beta = round(100 + 15z), then limited to 55–145, where round means rounding to the nearest integer. For R = 8, z ≈ 1.428 and Beta is 121. This is a conversion relative to simulated random answering, not a population IQ of 121. Applying the formula to the synthetic raw score of 18 gives about 195 before the cap and 145 after it; that 145 is not the population standard score in the synthetic result above.

The current result display does not treat this random-response Beta as a population standard score. It shows a standard score and percentile only when a separate population-reference result meets availability and eligibility requirements and includes the reference population and necessary norm and model versions. ## Difference Between Online Tests and Formal Exams

Formal cognitive assessment typically requires trained professionals, standardized administration conditions, age- and population-appropriate normative data, clear scoring procedures, and multiple subtests. The intended purpose also influences tool selection, as tools designed for educational support, clinical evaluation, and credentialing have distinct requirements.

This online practice form is easy to access and useful for recording performance on its reasoning tasks. Its limitations include variation in session conditions, a narrow task range, and possible effects of device use and familiarity. Evaluate a professional assessment by its particular instrument, administration, and evidence; online or offline delivery alone does not decide its quality. Even if two websites both offer an "IQ test," direct score comparison cannot be assumed.

The [Mensa Norway English practice instructions](https://test.mensa.no/Home/Test/en) distinguish online practice from professionally controlled testing. Its item count, timing, and score range belong to that tool and cannot be transferred to FermatMind. FermatMind's original 30-question result is not an official Raven assessment or Mensa certification.

## Why Can't It Be Directly Interpreted as a Percentile?

Percentiles require a clear reference group: who participated, sample size, when data were collected, whether the group represents the target population, and how age and language differences were addressed. Without this information, "you performed better than X percent of people" merely appears precise.

Reliability, validity, and norms cannot be substituted by a single "scientific algorithm." If current public data do not provide specific numerical values, it should be explicitly stated as Unknown or "specific numerical values are not provided in currently available public information," rather than estimated.

## How to Assess Whether an Online Test's Claims Are Credible

First examine six public pieces of information: what the test generally measures, its intended purpose, scoring method, reference group, known limitations, and what the results cannot be used for. If a page emphasizes only "authoritative, accurate, and rapidly reveals true IQ" without clarifying sample and usage context, readers are unable to verify those claims.

It's also important to distinguish between question difficulty and measurement quality. The appearance of difficult questions does not imply that scores automatically possess formal validity; displaying scores in decimal or percentile form does not mean a valid reference group exists. Test providers should inform users which facts are publicly available and which remain unknown—rather than using technical jargon to fill gaps.

FermatMind publicly describes an original 30-item form, an estimated duration of about 20 minutes, matrix and graphic-pattern tasks, and raw scoring and the random-response Beta calculation. Published algorithm parameters are separate from evidence about appropriate population norms, reliability, validity, and standard errors; one cannot substitute for the other.

Interpret the result according to the product details currently shown. If the item count, time guidance, task types, or result fields change, use the updated public description. Any future norms or research evidence would need to identify the applicable sample, method, version, and limitations; a generic "scientifically validated" badge would not be enough.

## How to Record a Useful Test Session

Do not save only the total score after completion. It is recommended to record:

- Answering date and environment;
- General sleep and fatigue levels;
- Whether interrupted;
- Which question type was most difficult;
- Most common erroneous assumptions;
- Whether your own schedule led you to rush;
- One strategy learned during review.

This information helps distinguish "current state," "familiarity with question types," and "practicable strategies," avoiding attributing all differences to a single score.

### Can you include results in your resume or application materials?

Do not present the result as a qualification in a resume or application. It is not an accredited credential recognized by the target organization and cannot replace formal evidence of capability.

### Will doing it a few more times be more accurate?

Short-term repetition may lead to familiarity and practice effects. When retesting, record intervals, environments, and test item familiarity; do not interpret changes in scores directly as changes in intelligence.

### Does a low score mean you're not intelligent?

No. A low score on one online practice set does not establish low intelligence; the result is affected by question type, timing, current state, device, and prior experience, and it does not comprehensively assess cognitive ability.
