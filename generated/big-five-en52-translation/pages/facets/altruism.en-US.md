# Altruism Facet: How Readily Do You Offer Help?

Altruism concerns readiness to help, contribute, and respond to others’ needs. It is not moral proof, unlimited self-sacrifice, people pleasing, impact assurance, or a requirement to abandon personal goals.

## Precise Definition

A stronger tendency notices opportunities to help and experiences helping as relatively natural. A weaker tendency may prioritize self-reliance, explicit reciprocity, or personal goals. Whether help is wanted, effective, sustainable, and fair remains a separate question.

## Parent Domain

Altruism belongs to [Agreeableness](/en/personality/big-five/agreeableness), reflecting interpersonal orientation toward assistance.

## Distinguishing Neighboring Facets

- **Altruism versus Dutifulness:** Duty follows obligation; altruistic help may be voluntary.
- **Altruism versus people pleasing:** Approval seeking can motivate help, but is not required.
- **Altruism versus Tender-Mindedness:** [Tender-Mindedness](/en/personality/big-five/facets/tender-mindedness) concerns sympathetic concern; Altruism concerns readiness to act.

## Behavioral Cues

Stronger Altruism may appear as offering time, sharing resources, mentoring, or noticing workload. Weaker tendency may appear as waiting for a request, protecting capacity, or emphasizing reciprocal arrangements. Resources, caregiving burden, culture, and power shape visible helping.

## Contextual Differences

Someone may help family extensively and avoid workplace favors, or contribute anonymously while declining emotional labor. Low capacity is not low care. A harmful request should not be honored merely to express a trait.

## Possible Expressions When Stronger

- Offering practical assistance without prompting.
- Sharing knowledge and access.
- Feeling satisfaction from contribution.
- Overcommitting or rescuing without consent.
- Neglecting personal needs and strategic priorities.

## Possible Expressions When Weaker

- Asking for clear requests and roles.
- Protecting time and resources.
- Favoring reciprocal or system-level solutions.
- Missing unspoken needs.
- Contributing reliably when expectations are explicit.

## Strengths and Costs

Stronger Altruism can support mutual aid and cooperative capacity. Possible costs include exploitation, burnout, or ineffective help. Weaker Altruism can support boundaries and self-direction; possible costs include reduced support or relational friction. Neither level determines goodness.

## An Original Case

A colleague offers one hour to teach a process, documents it, and declines to take over the whole project. Help increases capability without creating dependence.

## An Original Counterexample

A person rarely volunteers spontaneously but funds a shared service and honors every explicit support commitment. Low spontaneous helping does not prove selfishness.

## Self-Observation Questions

1. Do you help before confirming what is wanted?
2. Which requests exceed current capacity?
3. Does help build capability or dependence?
4. What motive is present: care, duty, approval, guilt, or reciprocity?
5. Can you decline without treating yourself as bad?

## Low-Risk Action Experiment

Choose one small requested help with a clear time boundary, or renegotiate one overextended commitment. Record effect on both people. The aim is useful, consensual help—not maximizing sacrifice.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Does strong Altruism require unconditional self-sacrifice?**

No. Sustainable help needs boundaries, consent, and competing responsibilities.

**Does weaker Altruism mean selfishness?**

No. Moral judgment and conduct require direct evidence.

**What relationships work well with a highly altruistic person?**

Relationships with explicit needs, appreciation, reciprocity, and respect for limits reduce exploitation.

**Can Altruism interfere with personal goals?**

It can when commitments exceed capacity, but trade-offs depend on choices and context.

**Can helping behavior be practiced?**

Specific noticing and support behaviors can be practiced. This page does not promise trait change.
