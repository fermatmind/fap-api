# Quick-decision quality rubric

## Reader value

A passing section lets a reader answer all four questions without reading another page:

1. What recurring work am I agreeing to?
2. Which observable behaviors or conditions make that work more sustainable?
3. Which tradeoffs should make me investigate further?
4. What can I try now before paying for training or changing jobs?

Require occupation-specific tasks or work products, a real work-context tradeoff, and an executable simulation. Structure may repeat across careers; reasoning, examples, constraints, and experiments must not be interchangeable.

## Scientific and ethical boundary

- Fit is a conditional hypothesis, not a latent truth about a person.
- RIASEC, Big Five, Work Styles, and Work Values are exploratory inputs. MBTI may support reflection only and must not be presented as predictive occupational evidence.
- Do not claim causation, employability, performance, income, promotion, exam success, or mental/physical fitness unless the exact claim is supported and appropriate for this section.
- Do not convert average occupational requirements into a diagnosis or exclusion rule for an individual. Mention accommodations or role variation when they materially change the conclusion and are source-supported.
- Avoid shame, fear, fixed-trait language, and protected-class proxies.

## SEO and GEO

- The title uses the reader's natural question and canonical occupation name; the first sentence gives the answer.
- Use specific occupational entities, tasks, outputs, tools, environments, and responsibilities naturally. Do not repeat the occupation name or target query for density.
- Keep each paragraph independently understandable, with explicit market/jurisdiction qualifiers where needed. This supports accurate extraction by search and answer systems without writing robotic fragments.
- Separate fact from interpretation. Prefer compact attributable statements over vague “research shows” wording.
- Do not duplicate FAQ answers, add hidden schema, invent high-frequency queries, or turn the section into a CTA. Visible content remains the source for any later structured projection.

## Differentiation check

Compare each draft with at least two adjacent or batch careers using the same reader intent. Review manually when any of the following is true:

- the occupation name can be swapped without changing the paragraph;
- the `suit` field is mostly adjectives with no task consequence;
- the `boundary` field uses a generic “high pressure / detail / communication” list with no setting or trigger;
- the `how` field is a career path, credential list, personality test, or vague instruction to “try the job”;
- sentences, examples, or clause order repeat across the batch without an occupational reason;
- the summary discusses pay, AI, or prospects instead of the core work bargain.

## Decision

- `PASS`: evidence scope is correct, all six fields form a coherent argument, the experiment is safe and executable, and the section is occupation-specific and useful.
- `WARN`: optional clarity or compression would help, but no material claim or reader-action defect exists. Under the Agent gate contract, WARN still stops that execution.
- `BLOCKED`: missing/incorrect evidence, market or occupation mismatch, deterministic/discriminatory language, unsafe experiment, misleading action, generic template copy, or conflict with the ownership map.
